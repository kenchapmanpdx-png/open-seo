## Residual weaknesses

- SUPPORTED: 6 of 40 deterministic findings (15 %) were false positives, after correcting 3 first-pass verdicts against the handbook rules (nwpt F29, love4paws F16, mobilepet F17 → TRUE). No verified false-positive cause repeats on a second verified site and each is S2–S4, so they inflate the Fix List more than the grade.
- SUPPORTED: brand-suffix (P4-01) false positive verified on love4paws (outside the 10 sampled): the majority suffix is a keyword tagline, and titles that start with og:site_name are flagged. Same finding on 5 other sites; nwpt's is TRUE, the rest not checked.
- SUPPORTED: nwpt's S1 (F01) is real for ClaudeBot, but its Googlebot half did not reproduce (spoofed Googlebot UA → 200 now). The S1 cap rests on ClaudeBot alone.
- SUPPORTED: 0 of 10 AI findings are false against their rubric, but 4 are partly overstated (nwpt F35 "#1" is attributed, love4paws F27 IBD is spelled out, theogdenver F28 home half, bluebonnet F14 "best course of action" / GAF). Each was counted TRUE. Fourth first-pass correction: bluebonnet F14.
- SPECULATIVE: NAP display-text variants alone (nwpt: one href, four text formats) open P5-08 although §25 names only the `tel:` format; not judged here.
- SPECULATIVE: "No About page" and `/index.html` hits on other sites share the same mechanism and may also be false positives; not fetched.
- SPECULATIVE: "Same text block" may count hidden responsive copies on other builders (Wix, Squarespace) too.
- SPECULATIVE: app-only selection leans on title matching against the last field report. A finding Semrush reports under another name could have been counted as app-only.
- SUPPORTED: sample is 50 findings on 5 small sites (10 per site, picked by me); rates are rough.
