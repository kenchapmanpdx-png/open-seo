## 0. Meta

- scope: business writing — articles, blog posts, web and landing copy, product descriptions, emails, business documents. Out of scope: student coursework, academic-integrity evasion.
- goal: LLM-written business text that reads and scores as human-written, keeps quality and factual accuracy, and meets disclosure law and platform rules.
- precedence: an opened-source value from a later run overrides an earlier snippet-only or secondary value; every overridden value is kept in §12.
- grades: E1 controlled test with control group or platform-owner confirmation · E2 ≥2 independent before/after tests · E3 one test, or correlational with disclosed n · E4 claims without data, vendor self-report.
- risk: OK · GREY (no explicit rule; deceptive intent or platform warning) · VIOLATES (rule or law).
- Vendor = an author or funder sells a detector, humanizer or writing tool tested in the study. Independent = not Vendor.
- `STALE?` = detector or humanizer test dated >6 months before its run date.
- (S) = seen only in a search snippet; not verified.
- "opened" in the source runs includes pages read through web-fetch summarizers, page extraction and research subagents; some table values were not cross-checked against PDFs (run assumptions: §15.3).
- IDs: F-xx = R0 findings · NA/NB/NC/ND-xx = run findings · B/G/V-xx = prompt IDs · rule IDs W/X/E/Q/C = this guide.

## 2. Writing rules — drop-in for a writing system prompt

### Do

| ID  | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Basis                     | Grade         |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------- |
| W1  | Load 5 passages of the real author's own writing, same genre as the output. Instruct: match sentence-length spread, vocabulary level and opening habits; do not copy content.                                                                           | F-01, NA-03, NC-15        | E1            |
| W2  | Emails and news-style copy: rely on W1. Blog and forum posts: few-shot imitation alone fails (authorship-verification accuracy 16.72–21.25% on blogs); edit voice by hand.                                                                              | NC-15                     | E1 `STALE?`   |
| W3  | Write practitioner-to-peer: no greeting or recap opener, no "Here's…", no balanced pros/cons scaffolding unless the brief needs it, no closing summary, no offer of further help.                                                                       | F-03                      | E1            |
| W4  | State the format explicitly: flowing paragraphs; no headings, bullets or bold unless the brief requires them; none in email bodies.                                                                                                                     | NC-07, NA-08              | E3            |
| W5  | Write fewer, longer paragraphs; do not give every point its own one- or two-sentence paragraph unless the format requires it. Trade-off: scannability of web copy.                                                                                      | NC-16                     | E1 lab        |
| W6  | Prefer short concrete words; replace abstractions with specific figures, names, dates and quantities from first-party data.                                                                                                                             | F-05, NC-10               | E3            |
| W7  | Put ≥1 first-hand, checkable detail in every section: named client, date, measured result or quote.                                                                                                                                                     | F-07, F-08                | E3            |
| W8  | Emails: ≥1 detail only the sender would know; sign-off in the sender's real voice; exactly 1 call to action.                                                                                                                                            | NC-08                     | E4            |
| W9  | End on a specific fact, number, name or decision, not a vague optimistic conclusion.                                                                                                                                                                    | NA-04                     | E1 `STALE?`   |
| W10 | Use at most one three-item list per 500 words; replace stacked triads with one specific item.                                                                                                                                                           | NC-04                     | E3            |
| W11 | Keep "may / might / could" only where a named source or stated condition supports it.                                                                                                                                                                   | NC-04                     | E3            |
| W12 | Allow a genuine question where the argument turns (LLM 2.28 vs expert 5.55 rhetorical questions per document).                                                                                                                                          | NC-04                     | E3            |
| W13 | Vary sentence length across paragraphs; variation only registers over several sentences.                                                                                                                                                                | NA-06, NC-10              | E3            |
| W14 | Match em-dash use to the author samples; without samples stay ≤3 per 1,000 words. Vary punctuation (commas, colons, parentheses, full stops) rather than banning one mark. Instruct, then post-check: some models keep em dashes under an explicit ban. | F-15, NA-07, NA-08, NC-06 | E3            |
| W15 | Write in a consistent first-person or house persona with situated detail.                                                                                                                                                                               | NA-05                     | E3 (dialogue) |
| W16 | Write for the expert reader, not the detector.                                                                                                                                                                                                          | F-07                      | E3            |
| W17 | Keep default sampling settings.                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | NA-11                     | E1 `STALE?`   |

## 9. Evidence — search and platform outcomes

- Google policy (F-08, NA-14, NA-15, NC-12, E1): generative AI use allowed; generating many pages without added value may violate scaled content abuse; manual fact-check urged, including metadata (guidance updated 2026-10-01, synced with SQRG). Spam includes "attempting to manipulate generative AI responses in Google Search" (clarified 2026-05-15). AI optimization guide (2026-05-15): fan-out variation pages violate scaled content abuse. SQRG 2025-09-11 edition: §2.1 defines generative AI as helpful but misusable; §4.6.5 scaled content abuse; §4.6.6 low effort / originality / added value → Lowest; no single rating directly impacts ranking. User-generated-spam guide update 2026-10-26 covers shareable pages / generated outputs. No statement on AI-text classifiers in ranking; no manual action citing AI content found. https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content · https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies · https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide · https://guidelines.raterhub.com/searchqualityevaluatorguidelines.pdf · https://developers.google.com/search/updates.
- Ahrefs (NA-16, E3 Vendor, correlational): vendor's own AI detector; title states "331k Pages Studied" (conflict with ~150,000 scored kept, §12); Google top-10 pages, 100,000 June 2026 SERPs; 1,000,000 pages sampled, ~150,000 scored (≥350 words); 5.3% of top-3 pages 100% AI; pages <50% AI held 82.2% of top-3; low / moderate-AI pages drew 2–3x the impressions of high / very-high-AI pages; indexation 49.28% vs 40.35%; detector misclassification uncontrolled. https://ahrefs.com/blog/google-doesnt-punish-ai-content/ 2026-07-27.
- Semrush (NC-13, E3 Vendor, correlational): GPTZero (unversioned) classification of 42,000 /blog/ pages from 200,000 URLs, 20,000 keywords (November 2025); position 1: 80.5% probability human vs 10% AI; purely AI pages took the top spot 9% of the time; AI share nearly doubles from position 1 to 4; plus survey of 224 SEOs; detector misclassification unquantified; Search Engine Land headline: human content 8x more likely than AI to rank #1. Semrush sells content tools and owns Search Engine Land. https://www.semrush.com/blog/does-ai-content-rank-in-search-data-study/ 2026-04-01 · https://searchengineland.com/human-content-ai-rank-google-study-473697.
- SE Ranking 16-month experiment (NC-14, E3 Vendor): 2,000 unedited AI how-to articles, 20 niches, 20 new domains (Nov 2024–Mar 2026); 70.95% indexed in 36 days; top-100 share 28% (month 1) → 3% (~2025-02-03) → 20% after the August 2025 spam update (began 2025-08-26); six of 10 spiking sites returned to prior lows; 66.9% still indexed at month 16; 1,092,079 impressions, 1,381 clicks cumulative; finance 9/100 and health 14/100 indexed; no human-edited or human control arm; unnamed AI generator; new zero-authority domains only; no sustained recovery by month 16. https://searchengineland.com/ai-generated-content-google-search-experiment-472234.
- LinkedIn (ND-03, E1): classifier "94% of the time" correct in initial testing (method and FPR undisclosed); AI content lacking clear perspective less widely distributed beyond the poster's network; action on automated comments; AI-assisted writing reflecting the poster's voice allowed. Announced 2026-05-20 (Laura Lorenzetti), Pressroom 2026-06-04. https://news.linkedin.com/2026/keeping-conversations-real-on-linkedin.
