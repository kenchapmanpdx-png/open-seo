Harbour boat tour archive

Our harbour tour leaves the north pier at nine each morning and follows the old shipping lane past the lighthouse, the grain terminal and the two wrecks that surface at low tide; the skipper explains how the port grew from a fishing village into a container hub over two centuries.

Tickets include a hot drink, a printed chart of the bay and entry to the small maritime museum beside the pier, where a volunteer keeps the logbooks of the pilot boats that guided freighters through the sandbanks before radar was fitted to every vessel.

Families with young children should book the shorter afternoon loop, which stays inside the breakwater and lasts fifty minutes, while the full morning route crosses open water for about two hours and can be cancelled when the wind rises above twenty knots.

Wheelchair users board by the ramp at gate three; the lower deck has level access, a heated cabin and an accessible toilet, and crew members carry a folding chair for anyone who wants to sit outside near the rail to watch the seals on the outer buoys.

Bring a warm layer even in summer, because the breeze off the estuary is colder than it feels on the quay, and keep cameras in a dry bag when spray comes over the bow as the boat turns at the mouth of the river for the run home.

Refunds are paid in full when the harbour master closes the channel, and tickets can be moved to another date up to two hours before departure through the booking office on the quay or by calling the number printed on the confirmation email.
